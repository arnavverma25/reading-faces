Chapter 8 of 9

The challenges

Even a perfect classifier would leave hard problems: delay, battery life, wrong ideas about emotion, unequal errors, and the privacy of the people being filmed.

3 minute readLimitations
Read the narration text

Chapter eight, the challenges.

First, speed. You might think the neural network is the slow part. It is not. A small network needs between sixteen and about two hundred milliseconds. The smoothing window and the time to say a word are each about four hundred milliseconds. So the way to feel faster is a shorter cue, like a tone or a buzz.

Second, battery. Process fewer frames when the face is steady, skip the classifier when the view is poor, and shrink the model with quantization.

Third, and most important: an expression is not an emotion. A study of professional actors found that they showed the same emotion with many different faces. So the device should describe what it sees, for example smiling, and leave the meaning to the user.

Fourth, bias. The training photos are mostly of young and middle-aged adults, so errors are not spread equally.

Fifth, privacy. The camera points at people who did not agree to be analysed. Everything should stay on the device, and designers have to check the law. In the European Union, emotion recognition at work and in schools is banned, with an exception for medical and safety reasons.

Speed: where one second goes

The network time is measured: 16 ms for the smallest model and 191 ms for the largest of the small ones, on a phone processor [9]. A new frame arrives every 33 ms. The other two parts are my estimates: a 12-frame smoothing window holds 0.4 seconds of history, and a short spoken word takes about as long again.

The lesson A network twice as fast barely changes what the user feels. The delay comes from waiting to be sure and from speaking. A short tone or a vibration saves more time than a faster chip.

Battery

  • Process fewer frames. An adaptive frame rate is reported to cut the computing by a factor of 1.5 to 40 [14].
  • Stop early. No face or a failed view check means no classifier run.
  • Shrink the model. Post-training quantization stores the weights as small integers. For its simplest form, Google's documentation lists a model 4 times smaller and 2 to 3 times faster on a CPU [4].

An expression is not an emotion

This is the deepest limit, and no engineering removes it. In 604 photographs, professional actors portrayed the same emotion with many different faces. Only fear, happiness and surprise were shown with moderate consistency, and viewers judged better when they knew the situation [19]. The training labels share the weakness: two annotators agreed on only 60.7% of AffectNet images [10].

What this means for the design Describe, do not interpret. The cue should mean “smiling” or “brows lowered”, not “she is happy”. The user knows the conversation and decides what a smile means.

Unequal errors

A 2025 study measured who is in four widely used datasets. More than half of the faces are White, there are about 50% more men than women, and people under 16 and over 54 together make up less than 5%. The two most accurate models in the study were also the two most biased [18]. A device trained on such data will err more for some partners than for others, and its user cannot tell.

Privacy and the law

The camera films people who did not agree to be analysed. So: process everything on the user's own device, store no video, and make it obvious that a camera is on.

Designers must also check the law. The European Union's AI Act prohibits AI systems that “infer emotions of a natural person in the areas of workplace and education institutions”, except for medical or safety reasons, since 2 February 2025 [20]. Whether an assistive device falls under the exception is a question for a lawyer.

Limitations of the reviewed systems, compared

SystemMain limitation
RAN [16]Single images only. One network pass per region; phone speed not reported.
Latent-OFER [17]Heavy. The authors note that the rebuilding step transfers poorly to unfamiliar data.
MAE-DFER [12]Too large for a wearable. Tested on movie and web clips, not a worn camera.
HSEmotion models [9]No special handling of covered or turned faces.
Mukhiddinov et al. [5]Needs a server. Synthetic masks, no timing, no user test.
Yalcin and Alisawi [6]Large model, not yet run on glasses. Big drop from its own test set to AffectNet. No user test.

The image and video methods are limited by hardware and test data, the assistive systems by missing user studies. All output an emotion word for what is only a face movement.
