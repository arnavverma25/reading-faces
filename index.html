CS 663 research tutorial

Reading facial expressions on a phone. In real conversations.

How mobile computer vision can tell a blind or low-vision person that their conversation partner just smiled, frowned or looked surprised, when the input is live video and the face is covered, far away or turned to the side.

Welcome to Reading Faces. My name is Arnav Verma, and this is my research tutorial for CS 663, Computer Vision.

The topic is previous work on reading facial expressions with a phone or smart glasses, so that a person who is blind or has low vision can know how their conversation partner is reacting.

Most online examples of emotion detection use one clear photo of a face looking straight at the camera. That is the easy case. This tutorial is about the hard cases: live video, expressions that change, faces covered by glasses, masks, hair or a beard, and faces turned to the side.

There are nine short chapters, an interactive simulator with a quiz, and an annotated bibliography. Every page has a recording like this one. The numbers in square brackets are links to the references. The whole tutorial takes twenty-five to thirty minutes. Press Start the tutorial to begin.

Why this is not the usual emotion-detection post

The easy version

Take one sharp, front-facing photo and print “happy”. Many blogs show this, and it works well.

The version this tutorial covers

A camera on glasses films a real conversation. The expression keeps changing. The partner wears sunglasses, has a beard, or looks away. The device has to be fast and quiet, and know when it cannot see.
