Read the narration text

This is the interactive page.

At the top is a simulator. It plays a twenty-second conversation in which the partner smiles, looks surprised, looks sad and then looks angry.

There are three coloured strips. The first shows what the partner really does. The second shows a classifier that looks at one frame at a time. The third shows what the device would tell the user after smoothing. A small triangle marks each moment the device speaks.

You can change three sliders: the smoothing window, the confidence threshold and the head angle. You can also add a mask, sunglasses or low light. The four numbers under the strips show how well the device did.

Try it without smoothing first and count the wrong cues. Then increase the window and watch them disappear while the delay grows. Then add a mask and see which expressions are missed.

The simulator uses an invented noise model. It shows how the method behaves, not how accurate a real system is.

Below the simulator is a quiz with eight questions and instant feedback.

What is real here and what is not The smoothing, the threshold and the quiet rule are the real algorithm from Chapter 6. The frame-by-frame classifier is imitated with invented noise: a mask weakens mouth-based expressions, sunglasses weaken eye-based ones, and a turned head weakens everything. So the simulator shows how the method behaves. It does not measure how accurate any real system is.

Five things to try

  1. Switch smoothing off. Press “No smoothing”. Count the wrong cues a listener would hear in 20 seconds.
  2. Find the smallest window that works. Slide the smoothing window up until the wrong cues are gone. What happened to the delay?
  3. Cover the mouth, then the eyes. Press “Face mask”, then “Sunglasses”. Which expressions does each one take away? Compare with the table in Chapter 5.
  4. Turn the head. Move the head angle to 45° and then past 60°. Watch the third strip.
  5. Make it hard. Press “Everything at once”. The device goes almost silent. Is that better or worse than guessing?
